The script is written to be said aloud, in short sentences, roughly 130 words per minute. Speakers adapt the wording; the facts and the references are the fixed part. Each section names the documents the presenter cites out loud, so that the script points to the architecture document, the complete diagram, the observability design, the 100-hour plan, the costs and the release notes. Every number is a case-statement figure, arithmetic on one, or a design target; say "design target" and never "measured". Lines marked `PENDING` are placeholders for unmerged deliverables (specifically B13 final validation).

## Cover and team slide (no own time)

While the jury settles, SR1 reads the title and moves to the team slide. Each presenter says their name and role, in presentation order: SR1, SR2, JR1 and JR2. This counts inside the time of slide 1.

## Part 1: SR1 (4:00)

**Slide 1 (0:45).** "Good morning. We present the 2026 update of the Aroma Pacífico coffee traceability pilot: 200 farms in Cauca, Huila and Tolima, three collection centers, 60 transporters and the delivery at the port of Buenaventura. The delivery is plan-only: design, reusable Terraform, cost and this defense. Nothing was applied or deployed. We are four presenters. We will sustain three things: the connectivity, the flight mode of the drones, and why the infrastructure code fits in 100 hours. Everything we say is written in the architecture document, and we will name the section as we go."

**Slide 2 (1:15).** "The 2020 design assumed data coverage on most farms and a device publishing straight to the ingestion point. The collection center and the port had no network. We kept the requirements that still hold and replaced the connectivity assumption. Now every flow has an exit protocol, a local buffer that holds at most 24 hours, and a defined behavior when there is no signal. Each center gets a segmented LAN and a 100 megabit WAN. The port gets no Federation equipment. And the solution does not depend on a mobile generation that the operators switch off. Prices, devices and services from 2020 are not a valid quote, so the costs are new. What we keep and what we retire is in section 13 of the architecture document."

**Slide 3 (1:15).** "This is the complete diagram, in the diagrams repository under complete. AWS is the primary cloud, in one Region, us-east-1. Azure is prepared as an undeployed export target for the open-format history and the drone objects, so we do not duplicate the platform. One organization holds a management account and five member accounts: security, network, production, test and development. At the edge you see the farm nodes, the drone ground station, the apps and the center LANs. The numbered badges D01 to D13 mark what the infrastructure code builds, and JR2 comes back to them."

**Slide 4 (0:45).** "Governance and security are applied from the root: service control policies, a tag policy, budgets and an organization trail. The tags feed the cost split by farms, drones, collection centers, trips and environments. Six roles have separate identities with least privilege. The base security module in Terraform modules adds eight rotated customer-managed keys, a staff Cognito pool requiring TOTP MFA, a field pool with passwordless codes, seven role groups and an identity revocation role. MFA is required for the collection center operator, drone operator, platform operator and the agronomist. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest and kept 5 years. The buyer sees only origin, processing dates, status and drying variables. If a node or a drone is lost, its identity is revoked immediately and it stops publishing. SR2 now takes the connectivity."

## Part 2: SR2 (5:00)

**Slide 5 (1:30).** "The rule for every flow is identity, not address. Farms, phones and drones hold no cloud address. They authenticate with a certificate or a token. That is why the pattern grows from 200 to 1,000 farms by adding identities, not subnets. A farm node publishes MQTT over TLS with an X.509 certificate to the regional IoT endpoint. Crop readings go every 4 hours, drying readings every hour while a lot is open. With a delivery route, the alert target is under 5 minutes from the out-of-range reading. This is a design target, not a measurement. The center networks are 10.41, 10.42 and 10.43. The cloud uses a separate supernet, 10.80.0.0 slash 13, with one slash 16 per environment, so nothing overlaps."

**Slide 6 (1:30).** "Now the case that matters: no signal. The farm node keeps an encrypted buffer of at most 24 hours. On reconnection it publishes a live reading first, then drains oldest first. The alert is raised at the drain, within 24 hours, and it is marked as delayed. For farms outside coverage we use the same node with a satellite IP bearer. That means no new radio base and no weekly visit. The transporter app stores an encrypted outbox. It records a position every 5 minutes, never drops a custody event, and thins positions before it drops any. The drone mission is loaded before take-off, at the municipal seat or the collection center. If the link falls, the flight goes on, the record waits in the buffer, and the fumigation cannot close without it. The full table is section 12 of the architecture document."

**Slide 7 (1:00).** "At the collection center one LAN pattern serves the three departments with two parameters: the center key and the second octet. Seven zones keep the reserved blocks, each with its own SSID, and the visitor zone has no route to logistics or management. The WAN is 100 megabits, with two IPsec tunnels to a Transit Gateway and a cellular backup. As arithmetic on an assumed 50 percent of clients active at the harvest peak, the center needs 68 megabits down and 17 up, and we plan 90. At the port in Buenaventura we deploy nothing. The shipping line keeps its telemetry, and the platform records only the delivery event, sent by the transporter app."

**Slide 8 (1:00).** "Every center has a perimeter firewall and the cloud has its own. The policy tables list source, destination, port, action and whether the rule stays in the pilot or is temporary. The data tier has no route to the internet and no center can reach it. We also chose not to buy a NAT gateway or a network firewall appliance: AWS services are reached through endpoints. The same logic of a mission that waits for its link brings us to the drones. JR1 continues."

## Part 3: JR1 (5:00)

**Slide 9 (1:30).** "The flight mode is combined. The case statement has five mandatory services, and none replaces another. Area mode delimits the plot and estimates the harvest. Precision mode confirms the alert, fumigates the focus and verifies the same plot. Only combined mode covers all five. The switch between modes is a pair of persisted events: crop alert raised, then focus confirmed. A farm is in area mode by default, and only a confirmed focus can lead to a fumigation order. A contracted certified operator flies. A Federation agronomist authorizes each fumigation as a human identity with MFA. This is ADR-002."

**Slide 10 (1:15).** "This is the path from the alert to a closed case. Four gates protect it. The order cannot leave without the plot polygon and a closed flight record. A fumigation stays open until the verification object arrives. Authorization is human, and no trigger can execute it. And if the link drops over the plot, the mission is already loaded and the record is published when the link returns. The state does not change until that record is persisted. The details are in the drone service document."

**Slide 11 (1:00).** "Inference is event-driven. Four functions, one per trigger: the telemetry batch, the focus confirmation, the verification object and the harvest estimate. The event-driven inference module implements this pattern: an SQS trigger queue, a dead-letter queue, a least-privilege IAM role and a Lambda function calling Amazon Nova Pro on Bedrock. Each function reads a persisted object, calls managed inference in us-east-1 and persists the result with timestamp, confidence and source object. The orchestrator of these capabilities is event-driven: the event bus and its rules route each mission to its function, the queues buffer it, and the mission state machine in the database is the source of truth, so no workflow engine is needed in the pilot. We train no model, and no compute waits for objects: zero EC2, zero containers, zero provisioned concurrency. If an invocation fails, the source object stays and the case state does not change. Everything is stored and processed in us-east-1, so objects leaving Colombia go to one named Region, and only screened crop images and alert-opening readings reach the model."

**Slide 12 (1:15).** "Observability has two SLOs. First, crop alert delivery: the share of alert openings with a first receipt within 300 seconds of the reading, with a design objective of 95 percent over 28 days. Second, sack lookup availability: requests answered without a server error or throttle, with a design objective of 99.5 percent over 28 days. Those objectives are our assumptions ASM-224 and ASM-225, because the case statement gives the 5-minute limit and the 4-hour recovery time but no objective. We also detect the five conditions the case statement names: silent nodes over 24 hours, undelivered alerts, flights without a persisted record, fumigations without closure, and failed invocations that did not change state. There are two dashboards, operational and cost. The design is in the observability document, and nothing is deployed. JR2 closes with the code."

## Part 4: JR2 (6:00)

**Slide 13 (2:00).** "Why does the infrastructure code fit in 100 hours? Because we limited it to what the case statement counts: modules, variables, remote state and an executable plan of one environment. We use Terraform, because the 28 binding rules are written for it. We plan one pattern environment, qa. The reusable pieces are eight reference modules, tagged up to v0.4.0: network, state backend, base security with its eight rotated keys, farm, telemetry ingestion, the generic inference function pattern, firewall rules and the collection center association, with 142 plan-mode test runs. Remote state is one encrypted, versioned and natively locked bucket per environment. Configuration and secrets are cleanly separated across dev, qa and pdn with example tfvars; credentials enter via GitHub Actions OIDC and variables via TF_VAR, keeping secrets completely out of the repository and out of state files. The diagram shows the thirteen boxes, D01 to D13, one per task. What we left out is just as deliberate: the landing zone is designed but not coded, we do not instantiate the 200 farms or the three centers, and the other three functions and the lookup are architecture only. Observability, release notes, costs, design system and this defense are separate deliverables outside the 100 hours. ADR-003 and section 2 of the plan hold the reasoning."

**Slide 14 (1:15).** "The numbers come from the 100-hour plan. Thirteen issues, B01 to B13, split into 19 parts, each with an owner, an order, a dependency and a box. The total is 98 hours with 2 hours of reserve. No task is larger than 10 hours. Four people at 5 hours a day for 10 days give 200 hours of capacity, and the plan books 49 percent of it. The schedule finishes at working hour 38 of 50, on day 8 of 10. The main risk was how to show a real plan without an account: the faculty advisor accepted the Floci emulator as the plan target. Status today, from the roadmap board: the CI pipeline is done, with a plan against Floci and an Infracost estimate, and so is the collection center association, whose network stack plan proposes 163 resources to add in qa with no change or destroy. That is B05 evidence, not the B13 result. The CD pipeline and its runbook are merged but the task is still open, and the firewall rules and the pattern module are in progress. The result of the final validation, B13, is PENDING: issue 33 in the roadmap. Say that it is not merged yet and do not describe a result."

**Slide 15 (1:00).** "Change is controlled. Every pull request passes gates, and the qa plan is saved. Production applies only the reviewed saved plan, after a named human approval, and never between 05:00 and 18:00 in harvest week, unless there is an incident. The release notes have two templates, application and infrastructure, each with a completed example. They say what changes, the window, the impact, the approval, the notice to the Federation and how to roll back. The examples are fictional and claim no approval and no notice."

**Slide 16 (0:45).** "Costs are fully quantified in the pilot cost document. A normal month of operations costs 2,707 dollars and 27 cents total, with AWS cloud workloads at 558 dollars and 94 cents, safely under our 650 dollar budget ceiling. A harvest month costs 10,638 dollars and 75 cents, with AWS cloud workloads at 573 dollars and 75 cents, under the 700 dollar ceiling. One farm's hardware CAPEX is exactly 370 dollars for the crop node, drying node and mounting kit, totaling 74,000 dollars across all 200 farms, with recurring field connectivity at 920 dollars a month for 400 nodes. Commercial drone flights are contracted per mission across seven annual campaigns: 8,500 dollars per harvest campaign and 45,250 dollars annually for 1,105 flights. During harvest, 108,270 function invocations and 420 model calls cost just 5 dollars and 21 cents in total compute and Bedrock tokens. Six architectural decisions avoid 47,532 dollars a year in OPEX and 230,000 dollars in CAPEX: using VPC endpoints instead of a Network Firewall and NAT saves 10,856 dollars a year net; CloudFront in front of the lookup saves 490 dollars; invoking Bedrock only on alert openings saves 632 dollars; dual-mode hardware saves 50,000 dollars in surveys and 34,560 dollars a year in satellite fees; contracting drones avoids 180,000 dollars in fleet purchase; and on-demand DynamoDB saves 993 dollars a year."

**Slide 17 (0:30).** "The design system covers all three pilot applications: the offline-first grower PWA, the transporter mobile custody app and the public sack lookup. Built from scratch in pure SVG, the brand features the coffee bean over Pacific waves with single-ink and negative variants. Four sack status badges and five crop alert severities use color paired with dedicated icons. To withstand equatorial sunlight, eight outdoor usability rules enforce a strict 7.0 to 1 Level AAA contrast ratio across all 48 semantic color pairs with zero failures, alongside 48 and 56 pixel touch targets and high-reflectance light backgrounds. The public lookup delivers strict data minimization: exactly four groups covering origin, processing dates, status and drying statistics, exposing zero personal data or farm coordinates."

**Slide 18 (0:30).** "To close: connectivity is solved flow by flow, with a buffer and a behavior for no signal. The flight mode is combined, with a human authorization. The code fits because its scope is one environment and one pattern. Some decisions are still open and belong to the Federation: the answers of the clarification session, the thresholds and the drone operator contract. In the team, B07, B09 and B12 are in progress and the final validation, B13, is pending. Every latency, recovery time and capacity we gave is a design target, and the tests are future work. Thank you. We take your questions."
